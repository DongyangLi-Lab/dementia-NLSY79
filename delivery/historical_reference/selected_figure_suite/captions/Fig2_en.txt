Figure 2 | Interview coverage and the joint structure of recorded phenotypes.

The 2010 six-year cohort is partitioned into complete self-interviews (blue; n = 11,835), incomplete self-interviews (orange; n = 2,426) and proxy interviews (violet; n = 398). Completeness requires every model input to be recorded. A, Joint age, height and weight matrix: diagonal panels show marginal probability densities, lower panels all individual observations, and upper panels 50% solid and 80% dashed highest-density probability-mass contours. The same 14,399 participants with all three measurements enter every matrix cell: 11,835 complete self-interviews, 2,177 incomplete self-interviews and 387 proxy interviews. The 260 participants lacking a required joint measurement remain in the cohort-level panels and source data. Marginal densities share a pooled absolute bandwidth within each variable; joint densities share a pooled full-covariance Gaussian bandwidth and grid for each variable pair. Densities integrate to one within groups; contours are not confidence intervals. B, Empirical cumulative distributions of the number of missing encoded model inputs. C, Empirical cumulative distributions of ln(1 + doctor visits), using recorded values; 586 incomplete self-interviews and 23 proxy interviews lack this measure. D, ADL-difficulty count distributions, including unrecorded ADL as NA; open and filled bar components denote participants without and with observed dementia, respectively, using full interview-group denominators. E, Missingness across all 60 original predictor groups in both baseline cohorts and both horizons. Virtual-variable columns are grouped back to their original predictors in E, whereas B counts encoded input columns. These are descriptive, unweighted coverage comparisons, without imputation or new group-comparison tests.
