Figure 3 | Age, body composition and functional context within the temporal cohort.

A–C, Joint age–BMI probability-density surfaces for participants reporting no difficulty using maps (purple), difficulty (wine), or not doing the activity (ochre). BMI is observed weight in kilograms divided by squared height in metres. The three groups contain 11,965, 1,567 and 777 participants with the required body measurements. A common full-covariance Gaussian kernel, evaluation grid, density normalization and z-axis scale are used; the pooled bandwidth reference includes all 14,399 participants with recorded age, height and weight. The three displayed densities include 14,309 participants with known map-use status; 90 body-measurement-complete participants with unrecorded map status contribute only to the common bandwidth reference. D–F, The same density estimates shown as 50% solid and 80% dashed probability-mass contours over all individual observations in the respective groups. Circles and diamonds identify participants without and with observed dementia. All body-measurement exclusions are tabulated; no tails are sampled away. G, The selected age–education composition, preserved at its original physical size: pooled half-violins, paired map-use/outcome boxes and all 14,589 observations with recorded education. Half-violins share a 1.479-year Gaussian bandwidth and width mapping. Boxes show the interquartile range, median and mean triangle; whiskers are the 5th and 95th percentiles. The 93 map-use-unrecorded observations remain gray points and contribute to the pooled age densities. The 70 education-unrecorded participants are retained separately. Horizontal scatter jitter is display-only. “Does not do” in G denotes not doing the map-use activity, distinct from difficulty or missingness. Density height is concentration rather than dementia probability, and all associations are descriptive within the selected, unweighted cohort.
